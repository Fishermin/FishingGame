# First pictures we need (Procreate, iPad)

This is only the **first batch**. Draw these and the fishing game on hole 9
can stop looking like colored rectangles.

Think **old Nintendo games**: chunky pixels, bright colors, no blur, no
photos. If it looks like a tiny sticker made of LEGO bricks, you are doing
it right.

---

## How to set up Procreate

1. Tap **+** for a new canvas. Tap the little picture of a rectangle, then
   **Create custom size**.
2. Set units to **Pixels**, not inches.
3. Turn on **Drawing Guide** → **2D Grid**. Zoom in a lot. Each grid square
   is one “game pixel.”
4. Use a **hard round brush** (or a Pixel brush). Size **1**. Turn
   **StreamLine** off. Do **not** use Airbrush, Gaussian Blur, smudge, or
   glow.
5. Pick **5 or 6 colors** and stick to them. Make a Palette so you do not
   wander.
6. Leave the background **empty / transparent** for people, fish, and items.
   (In Procreate: turn off the Background layer’s checkmark.)
7. Save as **PNG**. Not JPEG. JPEG makes muddy edges.

**Easy trick:** draw people on a **64 × 64** canvas (4 times bigger than the
game). We can shrink them later. Draw the pond picture on **256 × 224**.

| What | Canvas size |
| --- | --- |
| A person walking around the map | 64 × 64 is fine (game size is 16 × 16) |
| A fish in a fight | 48 × 48 (or 96 × 96 if that is easier, we shrink it) |
| A tiny item (rod, net, worms, camera) | 16 × 16 (or 64 × 64, we shrink it) |
| One square of ground (grass, water, sand) | 16 × 16 |
| The pond behind a fish fight | 256 × 224 |

## The first 10 jobs

Do them in this order. Jobs **1–10** make hole 9 look like a game. Jobs
**11–13** are extra people (and a dog) we also want drawn soon.

### 1. You (the kid who fishes)

A small person with a fishing rod. This is **whoever is playing**, so keep
the face simple: a kid or teen, not a famous person, not Catch.

Draw them walking:

- facing **down** (toward us)
- facing **up** (away from us)
- facing **left**
- facing **right**

Two pictures each way is plenty: one foot forward, then the other foot.
Like a tiny flip-book.

**Look for:** sneakers, a vest or hoodie, a little rod on their back or in
their hand. Friendly. Ready to sneak onto a golf pond.

### 2. Catch (the rival)

Another kid on the bank who already thinks this is **his water**. Give him a
**gold / yellow cap** so we can spot him from across the pond. He looks a
little annoyed, not evil.

Same walk pictures as You: down, up, left, right.

### 3. Zippy (the bait person)

A mysterious grown-up who always has the **best worms**. They hang out in
the trees and cattails, not in a fancy store. Maybe a vest with pockets,
maybe a box of bait. Kind of odd. Kind of helpful.

Same walk pictures: down, up, left, right. (They can also just stand still
if walking is hard. Standing is okay for Zippy.)

### 4. The groundskeeper

A golf-course worker who does **not** want fishing on hole 9. Uniform,
maybe a cap, maybe a rake. Grumpy, not a monster. He stands on the **sand**.

Standing still is enough for now. If you want, add him facing left and
right.

### 5. The pond squares (the floor of the map)

The map is a bunch of little squares that snap together, like floor tiles
in a dollhouse. Each square is **16 × 16**. Draw one of each:

1. **Deep water** — dark pond
2. **Shallow water** — a little lighter, maybe a tiny ripple
3. **Muddy bank** — where grass meets water
4. **Short golf grass** (the fairway) — neat and green
5. **Tall grass** (the rough)
6. **Sand** — the bunker where the groundskeeper waits
7. **Cattails / reeds** — tall plants you can hide in
8. **Trees** — woods Zippy hides in
9. **Clubhouse wall** — bricks
10. **Clubhouse door**
11. **Hole 9 flag** — a little flag on a stick

They should look like they belong in the **same** pond. Same greens, same
browns. No sparkles.

### 6. Bluegill (the first fish)

A **sunfish**. Flat, round-ish, small mouth, dark ear flap, olive and gold
and a bit of orange. This is the first fish the player ever photographs, so
make it extra clear.

Need **3 pictures** (same size, like a tiny cartoon):

1. hanging in the water
2. hanging again, but the fins moved a little (so it can wiggle)
3. a lunge / splash toward the camera

Draw it facing **left**. We put it on the top of the fight screen.

### 7. The starter backpack (five tiny pictures)

Each one is a little icon, like an app icon, but fishing stuff:

1. **Warped spinning rod** — a fishing rod that looks a bit **bent** and
   cheap, but you love it anyway
2. **A spool of 6 lb line** — a little wheel of fishing line
3. **Hand net** — a small net you hold, not a giant boat net
4. **Worms** — a handful or a little tub of worms (Zippy’s gift)
5. **Pocket camera** — a small camera. This is how you “catch” the fish: you
   take a picture, then you let the fish go.

### 8. The fight picture of the pond

A painting of **hole 9 at dawn**: golf grass, cattails, still water, maybe
the clubhouse far away. No people in this one. This sits **behind** the
Bluegill when you are fighting it.

Canvas: **256 wide × 224 tall**. Keep it simple. Sky, trees, water.

### 9. The photo frame

A **Polaroid** (the old white-border instant photo). Empty in the middle so
we can drop the Bluegill picture inside. This is the “I really caught it”
proof. Clubs stamp a photo, not a story.

### 10. The talk box

A rectangle that looks like a Nintendo text box: dark border, light inside,
and a little **triangle arrow** in the corner that means “tap to keep
reading.”

If you want extra credit (still first batch): a long thin **red bar** for
the fishing line (when it empties, the line snaps) and a **green bar** for
the fish getting tired.

### 11. Maeve (the rock lady)

A grown-up lady who talks like a movie mobster. Fancy **dark coat**, maybe a
**gold chain**, hands on her hips. She looks like she is about to say
“you’re in my office” — but her office is a fishing hole.

She is **not** a monster and she does **not** have a gun. She is funny-tough.
She wants **your spot** because the **rocks** there are the prettiest, not
because she wants to fish. (You can give her empty hands, or a little rock
in her fingers. No fishing rod.)

Draw her standing, facing **down** and **left** at least. Walking is extra.

**Look for:** coat, chain, maybe sunglasses on her head, a scowl that is
trying too hard. You should know it is Maeve from across the creek.

### 12. Pudding (her surprisingly nice dog)

Maeve’s dog. The friendliest dog in the whole game. Round, soft, big eyes,
tail always wagging. Maybe a **bow** or a too-fancy collar that Maeve bought.

The joke: Maeve is mad about the rocks. **Pudding already likes you.** Draw
one picture of Pudding sitting, one of Pudding walking, and if you want, one
of Pudding **sitting on someone’s shoes** (the happiest picture).

Pudding is her own dog. Do not just recolor a different dog.

### 13. A tiny pile of pretty rocks

A little heap of creek cobbles (gray, tan, one shiny one). Maeve’s treasure.
Same size as the other item icons.

---

## What “done” looks like

- Edges are **crispy**. No blurry halo.
- You can tell who is who from far away (gold cap = Catch, worms = Zippy,
  uniform = groundskeeper, fancy coat = Maeve, round waggy dog = Pudding).
- Bluegill looks like a real sunfish, not a shark and not a cartoon cat.
- Files are **PNG**.
- You did **not** draw a bucket of kept fish. In this game you snap a
  picture and put the fish back.

When 1–10 are in, hole 9 can look real. 11–13 can come right after. More
fish and more places wait until then.
